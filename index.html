Why I'm running

I'm concerned with the current state of our city's infrastructure. The issues are especially apparent in older neighbourhoods like Beaverbrook, but it's a city-wide issue. The City is clearly not able to keep up its infrastructure maintenance and replacement obligations. Streets that have never been repaved since they were built, left cracked and potholed. Sidewalks patched with asphalt year after year rather than being replaced. Renewal projects for water and sewer work repeatedly postponed as a "calculated risk", hoping that we might get a few more years of life out of them before they fail. But as we have seen, those infrastructure failures are not a risk we should be taking.

If we do not get a handle on this situation, the backlog will continue to grow and even our newest neighbourhoods will notice its effects. When it comes time for repair or replacement of newer assets, the list of work will have gotten so long there's no way it's ever getting done. It's time to get ahead of this and make the investments in our neighbourhoods BEFORE the infrastructure fails.

As for the current councillor, Cathy Curry has fought to block housing construction in Kanata. She was one of the biggest cheerleaders for the financially disastrous and unnecessary Lansdowne 2.0 project, she tabled the replacement motion to block Councillor Leiper from trying to reverse RTO for City employees, and she has voted in alignment with Mayor Sutcliffe more than any other city councillor.

When I delegated at Finance Committee last year about our aging water and sewer infrastructure and the city's lack of any plan to deal with it, one councillor said that he got more out of my five-minute presentation than reading the pages and pages and pages of words provided by the City. There was a lot of discussion around my delegation and the state of the city's assets from other councillors, but Cathy Curry called the discussion "doom and gloom". The point she wanted to make was that our $10.6 billion maintenance backlog can't all be that bad because Ottawa's credit rating is so great.

That's not someone I want to have making decisions on Council, and I think residents would agree.

I've never intended to run for council, I'm usually more of a behind-the-scenes guy. But when I saw Councillor Curry was likely going to run unopposed in this election, I felt I needed to put my name forward so that residents in Kanata North would have a choice for a different approach.

Background

Successive councils have neglected maintenance of existing infrastructure. Delaying a project is far easier for a politician to do than asking residents for the money to pay for it — just kick the can down the road for the next council to deal with. The problem is that these deferred projects have piled up into a $10.6 billion backlog. That road resurfacing or park renewal project that affects your life is now at the end of a very, very long list and councillors are forced to fight over scraps to get projects done in their wards.

We saw very clearly in the July 1st flooding events exactly what that looks like: outdated infrastructure and no plan from the city to address it. Councillor Curry voted to defer $2 billion in water infrastructure upgrades. I would have voted against that plan. We need to fund these necessary upgrades now.

I want tax increases to be as low as possible, but it's not as simple as just setting an arbitrarily low number every year. We need to build the city in a way that makes low taxes possible or we just end up paying high taxes for bad services. We need to look at how much it costs to provide services to neighbourhoods, and make sure what we build can cover those costs without needing high property tax increases. We need to stop cutting corners for short term savings on things that end up costing more in the long run.

What I'd do

  • 1.Support a dedicated infrastructure levy to address the maintenance backlog head on.
  • 2.Stop setting arbitrary tax increases and making the budget fit that envelope. Decide what we want to fund first, then determine what it costs.
  • 3.Reform procurement to consider lifetime costs. The lowest bidder is not always the best choice for the city.

I'll do what it takes to end this pattern of decline and help build Ottawa into a world-class city.

Background

Kanata North is quite unique as a suburb in that it has a fairly large commercial taxbase in the tech park. Unlike other suburbs that are primarily focused around commuting to downtown for work, Kanata residents who work in the tech park can benefit from much shorter commute times by having their place of work much closer to home.

But if we want to attract the young talent needed to grow the tech park it needs to be more exciting than office towers and surface parking lots. We need to work closely with property owners and reform our zoning to allow for more lively mixed use development within the space available. We need more opportunities for the small businesses that make a place somewhere people want to be.

I'm a big proponent of having the things residents need (employment, shopping, recreation) nearer to where they live. We spend far too much time simply getting to places. If you have shops and restaurants within walking or biking distance of your home, you don't even need to drive there. I would also like to see the city allow more mixed use development in residential areas rather than the very rigid separation we now see.

What I'd do

  • 1.Permit more mixed use development in the tech park, to attract more talent to the area.
  • 2.Legalize small commercial in residential areas by right: the corner store, the daycare, the physio clinic. It shouldn't be so difficult to start a small business.
  • 3.Build main streets instead of power centres, so a shop can live off its own neighbourhood instead of pulling drivers in from six kilometres away.

We can build something better than a sea of surface parking lots.

Background

We shouldn't be expanding the urban boundary any further. We are eating into forests, wetlands, and productive farmland with endless suburban sprawl. We need to stop expanding and instead grow inward. We have a very spread-out city with plenty of already-serviced land to build new homes where it makes sense, without eating into greenspace.

The Kanata Lakes golf course is not greenspace worth protecting and we should stop wasting money trying to block the redevelopment. By Cathy Curry's own words, "the mercury poisoning that's in the lands from the pesticides, from the fertilizers, from the insecticides, the fungicides that are put on golf courses was all embedded in all of the land." This is not what we should be putting our efforts into. By blocking housing construction on this golf course, we are losing real greenspace elsewhere as homes are built further afield.

The city needs to work with the developer to ensure as much real public greenspace is incorporated into the development as possible. Large public parks open year round, and trails and pathways for residents to get around linking the old and new communities as one. The outcome will be much better by residents and the city working with the developer than by letting the OLT decide what can be built there.

What I'd do

  • 1.No further urban boundary expansion.
  • 2.Put our money and effort into protecting forests, wetlands, and farmland, rather than on lawsuits over a golf course.
  • 3.Make it easier to build denser in already built up areas to protect our unbuilt ones.

This must be how we grow. We cannot afford any other option.

Background

People need viable alternatives to driving. If we keep spending our money widening roads, it just leads to more people driving and even worse traffic. And people need to be allowed to work from home if they're able to, not forced into offices downtown. They need to be able to rely on transit to get them where they need to go, and to safely walk or bike around their neighbourhoods.

The traffic issues in this city are a direct result of the failures in our transit system. People need a reliable transit system that they can count on to get them where they need to be on time. Traffic will only improve when fewer people are choosing to drive.

What I'd do

  • 1.Inexpensive transit priority measures. Bus lanes, signal priority, bus bulbouts. OC Transpo already has these improvements planned out, but the city needs to implement them. I will make these transit improvement measures a top priority.
  • 2.Better road safety and traffic calming. If more people are to get around outside of their cars, it needs to be safe to do so.
  • 3.I will push for Stage 3 LRT to Kanata.

Every person that isn't forced to drive somewhere is one less car on the road causing congestion.

Background

Young families are being priced out of neighbourhoods by protectionist, exclusionary policies. Strict zoning rules restricting what can be built and where have failed to meet the market demand and driven up prices. We need to allow more options for what can be built so people can afford to live in the neighbourhoods they want to live in.

Seniors looking to downsize are finding out that they would need to leave the neighbourhoods they've lived in their whole lives because we only ever permitted large single family homes to be built there. Many are choosing to remain in their homes rather than leave their neighbourhood, meaning new families that need a larger home often need to look for somewhere else to live.

As our city's population grows we are actually seeing many older neighbourhoods decrease in population as kids grow up and move out. The building forms in these neighbourhoods need to be able to change to keep the family orientated character of the neighbourhood intact.

We are in a housing affordability crisis, we need to give residents more options to find homes they can afford.

What I'd do

  • 1.Allow for more missing middle housing (duplexes, triplexes, fourplexes, garden suites) across the ward without a rezoning fight for every building.
  • 2.Stop fighting the construction of homes on the Kanata Lakes golf course.
  • 3.Rebalance development charges by lowering them on infill and raising them on sprawl, so the financial incentive is for developers to build a city that taxpayers can afford.

People need homes to live in, and we need to work hard to get them built, not fight against them.

Frequently asked questions

About me. Expand for answers.

Who is James Murchison?

I was born in Ottawa, my family moved from Kanata when I was little and I grew up in Carleton Place. After living in Toronto for a few years, I moved back to Ottawa and bought a home here ten years ago. I have deep family roots in Ottawa, and my wife and I are raising our family here.

My professional background is in dental technology, so not particularly relevant to municipal work, but I manage a large staff and deal with a lot of people's issues every day which is quite a transferable skillset.

Otherwise, I'm a huge municipal nerd. I follow city issues in Ottawa very closely: I read all the background policy documents and watch the committee and council meetings. I've delegated at committee meetings multiple times and have provided support and data for others who have delegated as well.

I'm an active member of my local community association, and I volunteer with a number of other city-wide groups such as Strong Towns Ottawa, where I dig deep into municipal data and policy to make complicated municipal policy more understandable to the public and even to City Councillors.

Why should I vote for you?

What I hear most from residents is that they pay too much in taxes for a city that doesn't work. I want to change that. My primary focus is on making the city work so that residents can see the value they're getting for their hard earned tax dollars.

Residents want the city to deal with affordability, transportation issues, community safety, protection of greenspace, and especially ensuring functioning city services. I'm not going to promise low tax increases like other politicians and then not be able to deliver on any of these priorities. I'll make sure residents see that the city is spending their tax dollars on their priorities.

The other candidates for Kanata North aren't offering anything new, I am. Ottawa should be a world-class city. I believe we can do better than the status quo.

What should the city do next about the Kanata Lakes golf course redevelopment?

The courts have made it very clear that the developer is allowed to build on this land. We need to accept that and move on. We can't keep wasting taxpayer money on this.

If we keep fighting this development, only the developer will be happy by the end of it. By not working with the developer to build a community that everyone will benefit from, we're letting them make all the decisions about what gets built. It's time to meaningfully engage in how this community grows, rather than keep fighting a losing battle.

I think this development could be a great addition to the community if we work to make it the best it can be. New families will move into these new homes. There will be new year-round parks for kids to play in, for neighbours to meet one another. We can't freeze a neighbourhood in time; cities need to be able to change and adapt or they'll fail.

How can I get involved?

Talk to your friends, family and neighbours about what kind of city they want to live in, what changes they want to see and who they think is best suited to make those changes. The other candidates for Kanata North are offering more of the same. I'm offering a change.

Voter turnout in Kanata North was only 43% in the last municipal election, which is way too low given how important the municipal government is in your day to day life. Talk to your neighbours and get them interested in how their city is run.

I'm running because I wanted voters to have a choice in this election. I think democracy is important and I want others to be engaged in this important election.

I'm not accepting any donations to my campaign. I don't want to waste your money on lawn signs and pamphlets. Just talk to people about the issues and vote for me on October 26th.

Vote for me.

I'm asking for your vote on Monday October 26th, but don't let the election be the end of your involvement in city activities. The municipal government is much more accessible to residents than the higher levels of government and your engagement can make actual meaningful change. The important decisions that shape this ward and this city get made in committee rooms with usually very little involvement from the public. Ten residents delegating to committee has a much greater effect than ten votes, so stay engaged even after this election. Hopefully I'll be there to listen.
